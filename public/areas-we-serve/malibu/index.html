What risks shape money decisions for Malibu households?

Pacific Coast Highway, often called PCH, is the main road through Malibu, a long, narrow city between the Santa Monica Mountains and the ocean. A few canyon roads, such as Malibu Canyon Road and Kanan Dume Road, climb north over the mountains to the 101. When a fire, a slide or storm damage closes PCH or a canyon road, getting home or to work can take hours longer.

In the east, homes start near Big Rock and Las Flores. They continue past the Civic Center and Malibu Lagoon, out to Point Dume, Zuma Beach and Broad Beach in the west. Some are beach houses on the sand, and others are ranch properties up the canyons. Households include families, retirees, people who split time between Malibu and another home, and owners who rent out a guest house. Small shops and restaurants gather near the Civic Center and along the highway.

Wildfire is part of planning here. The Woolsey Fire in 2018 and the Palisades Fire in 2025 both destroyed homes in and around the city. Bluffs and hillsides can also slide, especially after heavy rain. Some owners have seen premiums rise or policies dropped at renewal. All of this affects how much cash you keep, what coverage you carry and how you would pay to rebuild.

Services

Which services matter most to Malibu homeowners?

Strategic Risk Management

We look at your home, liability and income risks together. We check whether your dwelling limit, the most a policy pays to rebuild the house, would cover today's building costs. We also review how long extra living costs are paid and whether an umbrella policy, which adds liability coverage beyond your home and auto policies, fits.

Tax Planning

Insurance money after a fire raises tax questions few people expect. If the payout is more than your tax basis, which is roughly what you paid plus improvements, part of it may count as a gain. Some rules can exclude or delay that gain, and we plan around their deadlines.

Mortgage Loans

Questions

What do people in Malibu ask us?

What should I do if my Malibu home insurance is not renewed?

Start looking early, because new coverage can take weeks to place. Gather your current policy, recent photos of the home and proof of updates like a new roof or brush clearance. Through strategic risk management, we compare the coverage offered with what a rebuild might cost. We also point out gaps, such as limits on debris removal or extra living costs.

Is insurance money for a burned home taxable?

It can be. If the money paid for the home is more than your tax basis, the difference counts as a gain. Part of that gain may be excluded under the rules for selling a main home. Tax on the rest may be delayed if you rebuild or buy a replacement within a set number of years.

Can I keep my property tax base if I rebuild after a fire?

Assessed value is the value your property tax is based on. If you rebuild a similar home on the same lot after a disaster, the new home usually keeps your old assessed value. Space added beyond the old home's size may be assessed at current value. Under Proposition 19, you may also be able to move your tax base to a replacement home elsewhere in California within set time limits.

How much cash should a Malibu household keep on hand?

There is no single number, but local risks call for a larger reserve than usual. Plan for your insurance deductibles, a few months of living costs and the chance that you may need to stay elsewhere during repairs. We help you decide where that cash should sit so it is easy to reach and still earns interest.
