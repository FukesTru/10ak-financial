Los Angeles County

What should Los Angeles business owners know about the city's districts and rules?

Downtown alone holds several very different districts. Wholesalers, importers and garment makers fill the blocks of the Fashion District, and jewelers work a few streets away in the Jewelry District. Older warehouses in the Arts District now house design firms, restaurants and small makers. The Financial District has the office towers. To the west, Century City and the offices along Wilshire Boulevard hold law practices, agencies and investment offices.

A few freeways connect these areas. The 110 runs north and south through Downtown, and the 10 heads west to Santa Monica. The 101 runs through Hollywood toward the San Fernando Valley, and the 405 follows the Westside. Neighborhoods range from Highland Park, Eagle Rock and Silver Lake on the Eastside to Westwood and Brentwood in the west. You might own a shop, a practice or a service company. You might also be a working couple with children at home and a rental unit to manage.

Business and property inside the city limits come with a few added rules. The City of Los Angeles charges its own business tax, handled by its Office of Finance. The city also adds a transfer tax, known as Measure ULA, when property sells above set price levels. A transfer tax is a charge paid when real estate changes hands. Some addresses that say Los Angeles are outside the city limits, so we check where you are before we plan around these rules.

Questions

What do people in Los Angeles ask us?

Does my business need to register with the City of Los Angeles?

If you do business inside the city limits, you usually need to register with the Office of Finance, even if you work from home. The city may then charge a business tax based on gross receipts, meaning your total sales before expenses. Some small businesses can qualify for an exemption, but you still need to file on time to claim it.

How does Measure ULA affect selling a home in Los Angeles?

Measure ULA adds a city transfer tax when property inside the City of Los Angeles sells above certain prices. Those price levels are adjusted each year, and the seller usually pays the tax. Capital gains tax is the tax on your profit from a sale. If you are planning a sale, we can look at both taxes together and help you think through timing.

Can you help with a rental building under Los Angeles rent rules?

Yes. Many older buildings in the city fall under rent stabilization rules, which limit how much rent can rise each year. With rent increases capped, careful expense records and full deductions matter more. We can review your rental books and see whether a cost segregation study fits your building.

Can you keep the books for a wholesale or retail business Downtown?

We can record sales, purchases and inventory each month and match your sales tax returns to your bank deposits. In California, the Department of Tax and Fee Administration handles sales tax. Current books also show which products and customers earn the most, which helps when you plan the next season.
