Where do Costa Mesa business owners work, and what do they run?

The 405, the 55 and the 73 all pass through Costa Mesa, in the middle of coastal Orange County. The 405 crosses the north end of the city, and the 55 runs south until it becomes Newport Boulevard. Costa Mesa sits between Newport Beach, Huntington Beach and Irvine, with John Wayne Airport just past its eastern edge.

Each business area has its own character. South Coast Metro, next to the 405, has office towers, a performing arts district and large retail centers. The Westside holds older industrial blocks with workshops, distributors, auto shops and design studios. Along 17th Street, Harbor Boulevard and Newport Boulevard, you will find restaurants, salons and local stores.

Local owners run everything from apparel, surf and skate brands to contracting firms, restaurants and service companies. Homes run from Mesa Verde and College Park to the Eastside near Newport Beach. Public places like the OC Fair and Event Center, Orange Coast College and Fairview Park are part of daily life for many families.

What do people in Costa Mesa ask us?

Does my Costa Mesa business need a city business license?

The City of Costa Mesa generally requires a business license for companies that operate in the city, including many that run from a home. The city sets the fees and renewal dates. We add the license to your yearly calendar along with sales tax, payroll and state filings.

When does a Costa Mesa company need more than bookkeeping?

A common sign is a business that looks profitable on paper but often runs short of cash. Others include a large wholesale order, a new lease or a loan request. At that point, a forecast and a budget let you decide with numbers in hand. Our CFO advisory services are built for that stage.

Can you handle my personal return along with the business?

Yes. If your company is an S corporation or a partnership, its profit passes through to the owners' personal returns, so the 2 returns are closely linked. California offers an elective pass-through entity tax, often called the PTE tax, where the business pays state tax on the owners' behalf. In years it is available, it may let owners deduct more state tax on their federal returns, so we review it every year.
