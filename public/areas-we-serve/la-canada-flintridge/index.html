Los Angeles County

What is it like to own a home in La Cañada Flintridge?

La Cañada Flintridge is a small city in Los Angeles County, set between Glendale, Pasadena and the San Gabriel Mountains. Foothill Boulevard is the main street, with shops, offices and restaurants along it. The 210 runs through the city and meets the 2, the Glendale Freeway, on its south side. Angeles Crest Highway begins here and climbs north into the Angeles National Forest.

Most of the city is residential, with homes on large lots, winding streets and canyon roads. Descanso Gardens is here, and hiking trails start just above many neighborhoods. Households often include doctors, engineers, attorneys and people who run their own firms. The local public schools are a big reason many families choose the city, so college planning tends to start early.

Living next to the forest brings real fire risk. Large fires have burned in the mountains above the city, and after a fire, heavy rain can send mud and debris down the canyons. Homeowners in foothill areas across California have also had trouble renewing their insurance. Here a sound plan treats the house as both a home and a large asset that needs protection.

What do people in La Cañada Flintridge ask us?

Our insurer will not renew our policy. What should we do first?

Find the date your coverage ends and gather your policy, the notice and photos or video of your home and belongings. A gap in coverage can cause problems with your mortgage, so act before that date. Through our strategic risk management work, we compare what each replacement policy covers, including any wildfire limits.

How early should La Cañada Flintridge parents start college planning?

Earlier gives you more choices, though a plan helps at any stage. The FAFSA uses your income from 2 years before the school year, so the plan should look at that year early. We suggest a first review by the time your oldest child starts high school.

Can a fire affect my taxes as well as my home?

It can. A casualty loss is damage from a sudden event like a fire, and federal rules allow a deduction only in limited cases, such as a declared disaster. If insurance pays more than your tax cost in the home, part of the payout could count as a gain. Special rules for rebuilding or replacing a main home may let you delay or exclude that tax, and our tax planning looks at each one.
