Services

Which services do Pasadena residents ask about most?

Pension and Cash Balance Plans

A cash balance plan is a type of pension that can let a business owner save much more each year than a 401(k) alone allows. It may fit a Pasadena medical, dental or law practice with steady profits and an owner in their 40s, 50s or 60s.

Read more

Cost Segregation Study

Depreciation is a yearly tax deduction for the wear on a building. A cost segregation study sorts parts of a building, such as certain wiring, flooring and parking areas, into groups that may be deducted faster. Owners of older Pasadena apartment and office buildings may find deductions they are not yet claiming.

Read more

College Planning

We help you set a college budget and compare a 529 plan, which is a state-sponsored account for education savings, with other ways to save. We also show how the FAFSA, the federal student aid form, may count your savings. The aim is to pay for school without emptying your retirement accounts.

Los Angeles County

Who do we help in Pasadena, from Old Pasadena to Hastings Ranch?

Pasadena sits at the west end of the San Gabriel Valley in Los Angeles County. The 210 and the 134 meet near the Arroyo Seco, and the 110 ends just south of Old Pasadena. Colorado Boulevard runs through the middle of town, past the brick buildings and side alleys of Old Pasadena and on toward the Playhouse Village. Office buildings line much of Lake Avenue, and the South Lake Avenue district mixes shops with medical and professional suites.

The city has a long list of small professional practices. Physicians, dentists, therapists, attorneys, architects and engineers often run their own offices here, and some own the buildings they work in. Other owners hold older apartment buildings or small commercial properties that have been in the family for years. Salaried researchers, teachers and managers raising school-age children also live here, in neighborhoods from Bungalow Heaven and Madison Heights to Linda Vista and Hastings Ranch.

Pasadena also has many historic homes. Some owners hold a Mills Act contract, an agreement with the city that can lower property tax in exchange for keeping the house in good repair. If you are planning a sale or a big remodel, it helps to know whether your home has one. We can build it into your budget, and the city can answer questions about the contract terms.

Questions

What do people in Pasadena ask us?

Can a small Pasadena practice use a cash balance plan?

Sometimes. These plans tend to suit practices with high, steady profits, because the business must make set contributions each year. If you have employees, the plan usually has to include them, and their cost is part of the math. We work out the numbers with a plan actuary, a math professional who sets the yearly contribution.

Is it too late for a cost segregation study on a building I bought years ago?

Often it is not. The IRS may let you catch up on missed depreciation on this year's return. You do it through a change in accounting method, which is a formal filing to switch how you depreciate the property. Whether a cost segregation study is worth it depends on the building, what you paid and how long you plan to keep it.

Do you work with Pasadena families who do not own a business?

Yes. We work with salaried households on tax return filing, college savings, life insurance reviews and retirement planning. You do not need a business to get value from a plan.
