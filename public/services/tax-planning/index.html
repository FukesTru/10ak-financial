Tax Planning for Business Owners and High Earners in Los Angeles

1Oak Financial Group is a tax, accounting and financial planning firm in Monrovia, California, and its tax planning service lets you make key choices before the year ends. We look at your income, your business and your savings. Then we give you a short list of steps to take before the deadlines pass.

Where to begin

What should you do first to start planning your taxes?

  1. Find your last 2 returns

    Pull your federal and California returns from the past 2 years. They show where your income came from and how much tax you paid.

  2. List what is changing this year

    Write down any big changes, such as a raise, a new business, a home sale or a large stock sale. Each one can shift your tax.

  3. Gather this year's numbers so far

    Collect recent pay stubs and your business profit for the year to date. A plan needs real numbers to work from.

  4. Check your estimated tax payments

    Estimated tax payments are amounts you send to the IRS and the state during the year when no tax is taken out of your pay. Note what you have paid so far and when.

How we help

How does 1Oak help with tax planning during the year?

A tax return reports a year that is already over. Planning happens earlier, while you can still choose when income comes in, how your business is set up and how much you put toward retirement.

We meet a few times a year, often before payment dates and again before December 31. Each meeting ends with a short list of tasks and who owns each one. When the year closes, we send a written summary to whoever prepares your tax return, so the plan carries through to the return.

  • A running estimate of this year's tax

    We project your federal and California tax as the year goes on. When your income changes in a real way, we update the numbers and adjust your payments.

  • Business setup and owner pay

    A limited liability company, or LLC, is a common way to set up a business under state law. An S corporation is a business that has chosen to pass its profit through to its owners, who also draw a salary through payroll. We compare how your profit would be taxed under each setup, including the payroll and filing costs.

  • When income and costs land

    Some years it may help to pay certain costs before December 31 or to move income into January. Other years the reverse may make more sense. We look at equipment buys, bonuses, billing and gifts to charity with both years in mind.

  • Retirement plan contributions

    Retirement plans for owners can be among the larger deductions you control. When profits are high and steady, we may test whether adding a cash balance plan is worth its cost and its yearly commitment. This is a type of pension plan a business sets up and funds for its owners and staff.

  • Your investments and household

    Business choices affect your personal return, and the reverse is true too. We look at stock gains, charitable gifts and Roth conversions next to the business plan. A Roth conversion moves money from a pre-tax retirement account into a Roth account, and you pay income tax on the amount you move.

Situations we see

Which tax planning situations do we see most?

Owners of growing S corporations and LLCs

Your profit has grown, and you are not sure your business setup or your own salary still fits. The IRS expects owners who work in an S corporation to pay themselves a reasonable salary, so that number matters.

Partners and professionals paid through a K-1

A K-1 is a form showing your portion of the income from a partnership or S corporation. This income often arrives unevenly, which makes estimated payments hard to size without a projection.

Households with stock pay or large gains

Stock grants from an employer or a big sale of investments can push you into a higher tax bracket for a single year. A tax bracket is a range of income that is taxed at the same rate.

Owners getting ready to sell or step back

Selling a company, adding a partner or moving toward retirement raises questions about structure and timing. These are easier to handle a year or more before the deal.

High earners who do not own a business

Employees with high pay and retirees with large accounts have planning choices too. Roth conversions, the timing of withdrawals and gifts to charity come up often.

California rules

How do California rules affect your tax plan?

California taxes capital gains at the same rates as wages. When you sell stock or a building for more than you paid, that profit is called a capital gain. The state has no lower rate for gains held a long time, so the timing of a large sale matters.

California also wants its estimated tax payments in uneven amounts. The usual split is 30% with the first payment, 40% with the second, 0% with the third and 30% with the fourth. Federal payments are split evenly. So a schedule that works for the IRS can leave you short with the Franchise Tax Board, which is California's tax agency. Very high earners may also be unable to base their state payments on last year's tax.

LLCs formed, registered or doing business in California generally owe the state a yearly minimum tax. Larger LLCs may also owe a separate fee based on gross receipts, which means total sales before any costs. S corporations pay their own state tax at the business level. We count all of these when we compare business setups.

The state also offers an elective pass-through entity tax. A pass-through entity is a business whose profit flows to the owners' personal returns. Under this election, a qualifying S corporation or partnership may pay state income tax at the business level. The owners then claim a credit on their state returns. For some owners, this can ease the federal limit on deducting state and local taxes. The election has strict deadlines and depends on current law, so we confirm the rules each year before we suggest it.

California does not follow every federal rule. For example, it treats some write-offs for equipment and buildings differently. It also taxes health savings accounts, which are medical savings accounts with federal tax benefits. As a result, your state taxable income can differ from your federal figure.

Questions

What do people ask about tax planning?

When should I start planning for next year?

A good time is right after you file this year's return, because you can see what worked. Some useful moves, like a new retirement plan or a change in business setup, have deadlines well before year end. Starting in the summer or fall can still leave room to act.

How is tax planning different from preparing my return?

Preparing a return reports what already happened to the IRS and the Franchise Tax Board. Planning comes first and asks what should happen, such as when to take income and how to set up deductions. Many people use both, and we hand the plan to whoever prepares the return.

Do I need to own a business to benefit from tax planning?

No. Salaried high earners, retirees with large accounts and investors with big gains often have choices to make too. Business owners tend to have more of them.

What changes if my business becomes an S corporation?

Your profit is split into 2 parts, a reasonable salary paid through payroll and the rest, which passes to you without self-employment tax. Self-employment tax is the Social Security and Medicare tax people pay on their own business profit, and income tax still applies to both parts. In exchange, you take on payroll costs, a separate business return and California's tax on S corporations. We run the numbers before we suggest it.

Can planning help before I sell a business or property?

Yes, and starting early keeps more choices open. How a sale is set up, how the price is split among assets and whether payments come over several years can all change your tax. We work alongside your attorney so the tax side is ready before the terms are final.

What do you need from me to get started?

Your last 2 years of federal and California returns, this year's business numbers and recent pay records are usually enough for a first projection. If your books are behind, our accounting and bookkeeping service can bring them current first.
