Cost Segregation Studies for Rental and Commercial Property Owners in Los Angeles

1Oak Financial Group is a tax, accounting and financial planning firm in Monrovia, California, that guides building owners through cost segregation decisions. A cost segregation study can move part of a building's cost into earlier tax years. Before you pay for one, we weigh the fee against your plans and the tax that may come due at sale.

Where to begin

What should you do before you order a cost segregation study?

  1. Pull your purchase papers

    Find the closing statement, any appraisal and the date the property was placed in service. Placed in service means the date the building was ready to rent or use. These papers show what you paid and when depreciation began.

  2. Split the land from the building

    Depreciation means deducting part of a building's cost each year while you use it to earn income. Land gets no depreciation at all. In much of Los Angeles County, land can be a large share of the price, which leaves less building to study.

  3. Decide how long you plan to keep it

    The benefit of a study builds over years of ownership. If you may sell within a few years, tell us early. A quick sale can give back much of the early tax benefit.

  4. Bring your last 2 tax returns

    Extra deductions help most when you can use them right away. Your returns show how your rental income and losses are being treated today.

How we help

How does 1Oak help you decide on and use a cost segregation study?

The IRS usually has you deduct a building over a long, fixed schedule. For a residential rental, that schedule is 27.5 years. For an office, store or warehouse, it is 39 years. A study breaks the building into its parts, such as carpet, some lighting, parking areas and landscaping. Many of those parts may be deducted over 5, 7 or 15 years instead.

Your total depreciation over the life of the building stays the same. Only the timing moves. Larger deductions now can mean more tax later through depreciation recapture, which is tax on past depreciation that comes due when you sell. We weigh the early deductions and the later tax together, and we fit the result into your tax planning.

  • An estimate before anything is ordered

    We estimate how much cost may be moved, what the earlier deductions could be worth to you and what the study will cost. If the numbers look thin, we tell you before you spend money. Owners who want a closer look at a building's cash flow can pair this with financial ratio analysis.

  • Engineering work by qualified professionals

    Engineers or construction cost professionals do the study itself and assign each part of the building to its recovery period. A recovery period is the number of years over which a part is deducted. IRS audit guidance generally favors detailed engineering methods over rough guesses. We coordinate their work and stay your main contact.

  • A site visit or a records review

    Some buildings need an in-person inspection. Others can be studied from drawings, photos, closing papers and contractor invoices.

  • Separate federal and California schedules

    Bonus depreciation is an extra first-year deduction for some short-life parts of a building. California generally does not allow it, so your state schedule often looks different from the federal one. We prepare both.

  • Help with the return that reports it

    If we prepare your tax return, the study flows straight into it. If another preparer files for you, we hand over the schedules and any forms they will need.

  • A plan for the day you sell

    We sketch out what a sale, a long hold or a like-kind exchange could mean for recapture. A like-kind exchange lets you trade one investment property for another and put off the tax, if the rules are met.

Situations we see

Which property situations do we see most often?

Larger commercial buildings

Offices, retail space, warehouses and medical or dental buildings. A larger cost basis gives a study more to work with. Cost basis is generally what you paid for the building plus improvements.

Apartment buildings held for the long run

Multifamily owners who plan to keep the property for years and who can use the extra deductions as they arrive.

Short-term rentals you run yourself

When average guest stays are short and you take an active part in running the property, the losses may be treated differently. We check those facts before anyone counts on the deduction.

A building you bought years ago

A look-back study can often claim missed depreciation in the current year. It uses an accounting method change, an IRS filing that switches how you depreciate the property, so old returns usually stay as they are.

A large renovation or tenant build-out

New improvements, including work to fit out space for a tenant, can be studied on their own. Older parts that were torn out during the work may also affect your depreciation.

Times when a study may not pay off

A sale in the near future can undo much of the tax benefit through recapture. A price that is mostly land, a modest building or losses you cannot use for years can also make the fee hard to justify.

California rules

How do California and federal rules change the math on a study?

California generally does not follow federal bonus depreciation. So the state deduction from a study is usually smaller in the first year and spread over more years. The federal bonus percentage allowed has changed in recent years, so we confirm current law for the dates your property was bought and placed in service.

Rental losses are generally passive. Passive means the losses can usually offset only other passive income, such as profit from other rentals. Some real estate professionals and some short-term rental owners fall under exceptions. A small allowance for owners who take an active part phases out at higher incomes. Losses you cannot use carry forward and may be released when you sell.

At sale, gain tied to 5-year and 7-year parts is generally taxed as ordinary income. Ordinary income is taxed at the same rates as wages. Depreciation on the building itself has its own federal rate. That rate can be higher than the long-term capital gains rate, the lower rate that usually applies to assets held more than a year. California taxes capital gains as ordinary income, so your state result will differ from the federal one.

Questions

What do people ask about cost segregation study?

What is depreciation recapture, and why does it matter here?

Recapture is tax you may owe on past depreciation when you sell the property. A study speeds up depreciation, so it can also raise the amount subject to recapture. Gain tied to the 5-year and 7-year parts a study finds is generally taxed as ordinary income. That is why we plan your exit along with the study.

Can I order a study on a building I have owned for years?

Often, yes. A look-back study can usually claim the missed depreciation in the current year through an accounting method change filed on IRS Form 3115. How much you can catch up depends on when you bought the building and what it contains. Some cases still call for amended returns, so we confirm the right approach first.

Does bonus depreciation make a study worth more?

It can on your federal return. Bonus depreciation may let you deduct a large share of the short-life parts in the first year. The percentage allowed has changed in recent years and depends on your dates, so we confirm current law before relying on it. California generally does not allow it.

Can the extra deductions offset my salary or business income?

Sometimes, but often not right away. Rental losses are usually passive, so they mainly offset passive income. Real estate professional status or a short-term rental exception can change that. Losses you cannot use now carry forward to later years.

Which records does an engineer need for a study?

Usually the closing statement, any appraisal, building plans and construction invoices. For a newer building, the contractor's cost breakdown is very useful. We gather the papers from you and send them on, so you have one point of contact.
