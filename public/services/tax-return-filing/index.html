Tax Return Filing for Individuals and Business Owners in Los Angeles

1Oak Financial Group is a tax, accounting and financial planning firm in Monrovia, California, that prepares and files federal and California tax returns. We work with families, self-employed people, business owners and investors. You get a clear list of what to send, a review of the draft before you sign and plain answers when something looks off.

Where to begin

What should you gather first before we prepare your return?

  1. Find last year's returns

    Start with your federal and California returns from last year. They show carryovers, such as losses you could not use yet, and any tax you prepaid during the year.

  2. Collect your tax forms as they arrive

    Watch for W-2s, which show your wages and the tax withheld, and 1099s, which report other income such as interest or contract pay. Also watch for K-1s, which report your share of income from a partnership, a trust or a company you partly own.

  3. Add records for a business or rental

    If you own a business or a rental, bring a profit and loss statement or a simple list of income and costs for each one. A profit and loss statement shows what came in, what went out and what was left.

  4. Tell us what changed

    Let us know about a move, a new child, a home sale, a new business or a large stock sale. Changes like these often add forms to your return.

Situations we see

Which tax returns do we prepare most often?

Individual and family returns

Form 1040 for the IRS and Form 540 for California. These cover wage earners, retirees and households with investment, rental or retirement income.

Self-employed and single-owner returns

Schedule C reports business profit for consultants, freelancers and other sole owners. It also leads to self-employment tax, the Social Security and Medicare tax owed on your own business earnings.

S corporation returns

An S corporation is a corporation that has elected to have its profit taxed on its owners' personal returns. We prepare federal Form 1120-S and California Form 100S and issue a K-1 to each owner. We also check that salary and payouts are recorded the same way in every place.

Partnership and LLC returns

An LLC, or limited liability company, is a business formed under state law that can be taxed in several ways. We prepare federal Form 1065 and the matching state return, which is Form 565 for partnerships or Form 568 for LLCs. A single-member LLC in California usually still files Form 568, even when it files no separate federal return.

Investor and rental returns

Brokerage forms, fund K-1s and rental properties go on these returns. Rentals are reported on Schedule E with depreciation, which spreads a building's cost over many years as a yearly deduction. Owners of larger buildings may want to look at a cost segregation study, which sorts parts of a building into shorter write-off periods.

Extensions, amended returns and past years

We file extensions when a form is still missing and prepare amended returns to fix errors. We also help you catch up on years that were never filed.

How we help

How does 1Oak prepare and file your tax return?

Returns get harder once you add a business, several K-1s, a rental or a brokerage account with many trades. Each one brings more forms, more dates and more places for a number to go in the wrong spot.

We prepare your federal and California returns and walk you through the draft before anything is filed. If the return shows a large balance due or a business setup that no longer fits, we tell you. You can then carry that into year-round tax planning if you choose.

  • A checklist built from your last return

    Early in the season, we send a list of the forms and statements we expect, based on what you filed last year.

  • Review as your documents come in

    We look over each form when it arrives and follow up on anything missing. If a form is running late, we tell you early whether to request an extension, which gives you more time to file.

  • A walk-through of the draft

    Before you sign, we go over what changed from last year, what you owe or may get back, and what to plan for next year.

  • E-filing and confirmation

    After you sign the e-file forms, we send your federal and California returns electronically and confirm when each one is accepted. You get final copies for your records.

  • Next year's payment schedule

    If tax is not taken out of all your income, you may need to pay estimated taxes. These are payments you make to the IRS and to California through the year to cover that income. When they apply, we give you a schedule for the coming year.

  • Help when a tax notice arrives

    A letter may come from the IRS or from the Franchise Tax Board, the agency that collects California income tax. We help you read it, gather the records it asks about and plan a reply. Some matters need a credentialed representative to speak for you. That means a CPA, which is a state-licensed accountant, an enrolled agent, who is licensed by the IRS, or an attorney. We tell you plainly when that is the case.

California rules

How do California rules affect your tax return?

California residents with income above the state's filing threshold generally must file a state return. It goes in along with your federal one. These returns often differ, because California does not follow every federal rule. Some income and deductions are treated one way by the IRS and another way by the state.

An extension moves the date you must file, while the date to pay stays the same. The IRS grants an automatic 6-month extension to file when you request it by the deadline. California gives individuals 6 extra months to file without a separate request. Tax you owe is still due on the original date, and unpaid tax usually builds interest and may bring penalties after that.

The IRS and the Franchise Tax Board each send their own letters, with their own reply dates. A change to your federal return can lead to a state notice later. So when a letter arrives, we look at both returns.

Questions

What do people ask about tax return filing?

What happens if a K-1 arrives after the deadline?

Partnership and fund K-1s often arrive late, and brokers often send corrected 1099s weeks after the first ones. If a form is still missing near the deadline, we usually suggest an extension. We also estimate what you owe so it can be paid on time. Once the final form arrives, we finish the return, which often avoids amending it later.

Can you file returns for years I missed?

Yes, and missing W-2s and 1099s can often be filled in from IRS wage and income transcripts. We prepare each missed year and work forward to the current one. Filing sooner is usually wiser, since the penalty for not filing is often larger than the penalty for paying late. A refund on an old return is often lost once the claim window closes, usually 3 years after the original due date.

What should I do if I get an IRS notice?

Do not ignore it, and note the reply date printed on the letter. Send us a copy right away. We help you understand what the notice asks, gather the records and plan a response. If the matter needs a CPA, an enrolled agent or an attorney to represent you, we will say so plainly.

How do amended returns work?

If you find a missed form or deduction after you file, an amended return corrects it. The federal fix goes on Form 1040-X, and California usually needs its own amended return when the change affects your state tax. Refund claims have time limits, so raise a possible error as soon as you notice it.

Can you prepare my business and personal returns together?

Yes. A partnership or S corporation return creates the K-1 that feeds your personal return. Preparing both in the same place keeps the numbers consistent. It also gives us the full picture when we suggest changes for next year.

What if my business books are not ready at tax time?

A business return starts from your books, so they need to be current first. If they are behind, we can catch them up through our accounting and bookkeeping work. An extension may give the time needed to do this carefully.
