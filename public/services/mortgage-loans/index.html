Mortgage Loan Planning for Homebuyers in Los Angeles

1Oak Financial Group is a tax, accounting and financial planning firm in Monrovia, California, offering help as you plan and compare home loans. We do not lend money. We look at lender offers with your tax return and cash flow in view, so the financing you choose can fit the rest of your plan.

Where to begin

What should you do first before you apply for a mortgage?

  1. Gather 2 years of tax returns

    Lenders often ask for 2 years of personal returns, plus business returns if you own a company. Have recent pay stubs or a year-to-date profit and loss statement ready too. That statement is a report of your business income and costs so far this year.

  2. Choose a payment you can live with

    Start with what you can pay each month without strain, then work back to a price range. Count property tax, insurance and any homeowners association dues. Do this before a lender tells you how much you qualify for.

  3. Decide how much cash to keep after closing

    Pick a down payment, and also pick an amount of cash you will leave untouched. Lenders often look for reserves, which are savings you still have after the loan closes. Business owners may want extra set aside for slow months and tax payments.

  4. Review your credit and your debts

    Check your credit reports from the national credit bureaus and dispute any errors. Then list your monthly debt payments, such as car loans and credit cards. Lenders use them to figure your debt-to-income ratio, which compares your monthly debt payments, including the new loan, with your monthly income before tax.

Situations we see

Which mortgage situations do we see most?

Move-up buyers

You are selling one home and buying another. The timing, cash for a down payment before your sale closes, and a larger loan all need to fit together.

Owners whose returns show less income than they live on

Business deductions can make your taxable income look smaller than your real cash flow. We help you see what a lender is likely to count, ideally a year or more before you apply.

Homeowners weighing a refinance

You want to know if a lower rate, a shorter term or cash for a remodel is worth the closing costs. We work out how long it would take to earn those costs back.

Rental property investors

You are adding a rental and want to see how another loan changes your debt and your savings. We look at how the new payment fits with the rent you expect and the reserves you keep.

Buyers near the jumbo line

Your price puts the loan close to the conforming limit. A larger down payment may keep you under it, and we compare that choice with keeping more cash on hand.

How we help

How does 1Oak help you plan and compare a mortgage?

A lender decides whether to approve your loan and sets the rate. We do not lend, so our work happens before and around that decision. We help you plan how much to borrow, get your income records in order, and compare the offers you receive.

We also handle taxes, so we can spot when a write-off that trims your tax bill may also shrink the income a lender counts. We talk through that trade-off before you file or apply, as part of your tax planning.

  • Purchase planning

    We estimate a payment range, compare down payment choices and list the papers a lender is likely to want. A preapproval is a lender's written estimate of how much it may lend you after a first look at your finances. Getting ready early can make that step go more smoothly.

  • Refinance review

    A refinance replaces your current loan with a new one. A rate-and-term refinance changes the rate or the length, while a cash-out refinance borrows more than you owe and pays you the difference. We compare both with keeping the loan you have, closing costs included.

  • Jumbo loans

    A jumbo loan is a mortgage larger than the conforming loan limit. That limit is the largest loan the government-sponsored mortgage companies will buy, and it is set each year by county. Los Angeles County has a higher limit than most of the country. Jumbo lenders often ask for more savings and fuller paperwork.

  • Self-employed income records

    Lenders usually average income from your recent tax returns. We help you see how your returns may read to an underwriter, the person at the lender who reviews your file. We also help organize K-1s and profit and loss statements. A K-1 is the tax form that reports your share of income from a business you co-own.

  • Rental property loans

    Loans for rentals are usually priced and reviewed differently from a loan on a home you live in. They often need a larger down payment. We test how rent, vacancy and costs hold up, and whether a cost segregation study makes sense after you buy. That study sorts a building's parts so some of them can be written off sooner.

  • Comparing offers line by line

    Offers with similar rates can carry very different fees. Points are fees you pay up front to lower your rate, and lender credits lower your closing costs in exchange for a higher rate. We line up each offer so you can see the full cost over the time you expect to keep the loan.

California rules

How do California rules affect your mortgage?

If you itemize, meaning you list your deductions instead of taking a flat amount, home loan interest may be deductible. Federal law caps the loan amount that qualifies. California sets its own limits, and they do not match the federal ones in every case. Interest on a home equity loan, which borrows against the value you own in your home, has its own rules too.

California property tax is generally based on what you paid for the home, under Proposition 13. Yearly increases on that value are limited. When you buy, the county usually sets a new taxable value and may send a supplemental tax bill after closing. We build that bill into your cash plan.

Conforming loan limits for Los Angeles County and nearby counties change each year. Lenders must also follow federal rules that require them to check that you can repay. We confirm the current figures for your property before you apply.

Questions

What do people ask about mortgage loans?

What credit and income do I need for a mortgage?

There is no single cutoff, because it depends on the loan program, the property and your down payment. Lenders look at your credit history along with your debt-to-income ratio. Many also check your reserves, and jumbo loans often ask for more of them.

When does a refinance make sense?

Start with the break-even point. Divide your total closing costs by your monthly savings to see how many months it takes to earn the cost back. If you expect to keep the loan well past that point, a refinance may be worth a closer look. A new 30-year term can lower your payment while raising the total interest you pay.

Can I qualify for a mortgage if I am self-employed?

Many self-employed people do, though the paperwork is heavier. Lenders usually average income from your tax returns, so deductions that lower your tax can also lower the income they count. Some programs use bank statements instead, usually at different pricing. We help you weigh these trade-offs with your tax plan in view.

Should I put more money down or keep more cash?

A larger down payment can lower your payment and may help you avoid private mortgage insurance. That is a monthly charge some loans add when your down payment is below a set level. Cash in a house is hard to reach later, so business owners often keep more on hand. We look at your reserves and goals before suggesting a split.

Should I choose a fixed or adjustable rate?

A fixed-rate loan keeps the same rate and the same monthly loan payment for the whole term. An adjustable-rate mortgage usually starts with a fixed period, then changes based on a market index within caps written into the loan. It can fit if you plan to sell or refinance before the fixed period ends. It also shifts the risk of higher rates onto you.

Does 1Oak set rates or approve loans?

No. We do not lend money, set rates or approve loans. The lender makes those decisions based on its own review of you and the property. Our part is to help you present your finances clearly and compare offers by their full cost.
