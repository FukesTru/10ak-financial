For business owners

Accounting and Bookkeeping for Business Owners in Los Angeles

1Oak Financial Group is a tax, accounting and financial planning firm in Monrovia, California, whose bookkeeping service keeps business books current every month. We check your records against your bank statements and send simple reports you can read. That gives you real numbers for daily decisions, for your lender and for tax time.

Where to begin

What should you do first to get your books in order?

  1. Keep business and personal spending apart

    Use a separate bank account and card only for the business. Mixed spending makes clean books much harder to keep.

  2. Download your statements

    Gather bank, credit card and loan statements for every month you need covered. These are the records we use to check each entry.

  3. List every account the business uses

    Write down each bank, card, loan and payroll account. Note who can log in today, so access can be set up the right way.

  4. Find the last month that was fully up to date

    Tell us the last month your books were complete. That shows whether to start with a catch-up project or go straight to monthly work.

How we help

How does 1Oak keep your books current each month?

Many owners open their books only when a lender or a tax preparer asks for them. The rest of the year, choices get made from the bank balance and a rough sense of which months went well.

We take over the monthly routine. We record each transaction, close the month and send your reports with a short note on what changed. We work in cloud accounting software, and we can usually work in the system you already use. Most of the work happens remotely, with reviews by video or at our Monrovia office.

  • Recording transactions

    A bank feed is a secure link that copies transactions from your bank into your accounting system. We sort each transaction into the right account and attach receipts where they matter. Questions for you come in a single short list.

  • Bank and card reconciliations

    A reconciliation is a check that your books match your bank or card statement line by line. We do this for every account each month, so missing or doubled entries get caught while they are easy to trace.

  • Monthly reports in plain English

    You get a profit and loss statement, a balance sheet and a cash flow statement. The first shows whether you made money, and the second shows what the business owns and owes. The cash flow statement explains why profit and your bank balance rarely match.

  • Payroll records

    We work with your payroll provider to record wages, employer taxes and benefits correctly. We also check that payroll reports and your books agree.

  • Contractor tracking for 1099s

    A Form W-9 is the form a contractor fills out with their name and tax ID number. We help you collect one from each contractor, track what you pay them and flag who may need a Form 1099 before the January 31 deadline. A 1099 reports those payments to the contractor and the IRS.

  • A year-end package for your tax return

    After December closes, we finish the year and send statements and schedules to whoever prepares your return. If that is us, our tax return filing work starts from the same books.

California rules

How do California and federal rules affect your books?

Books can be kept on a cash basis or an accrual basis. Cash basis records income when money comes in and costs when you pay them. Accrual basis records income when you earn it and costs when you owe them, even if no money has moved yet. Federal tax rules may limit which method a business can use, based on things like its size and whether it holds inventory.

Federal rules require businesses to report many payments to contractors on Form 1099. California adds its own steps. You may need to report new independent contractors to the Employment Development Department, the state agency that handles payroll taxes. You may also need to hold back state tax on some payments to people who live outside California.

Monthly bookkeeping is different from an audit or a review, which are formal checks of your statements by an outside accountant. If a lender or investor asks for one, a licensed CPA firm, meaning a firm of state-licensed accountants, must do that work. Clean monthly books can make that process go more smoothly.

Questions

What do people ask about accounting and bookkeeping?

Can you fix books that are months or years behind?

Yes. We start with bank and card statements for each missing month and rebuild the books one month at a time. If earlier entries landed in the wrong accounts, we correct them so the year-end numbers can be relied on.

What accounting software do you use?

We work in cloud accounting software that connects to your bank and card accounts. If you already use a cloud system that fits your business, we can usually work in it. If not, we can help you choose one and move your history over.

How often will I get financial statements?

You get them monthly, on a schedule we agree on at the start, once that month's bank and card statements are out. Each set includes a profit and loss statement, a balance sheet and a cash flow statement. A short note points out what stood out that month.

Do you run payroll?

We work alongside your payroll provider instead of replacing it. We record each payroll run in your books, check payroll tax balances and flag anything that does not match.

What will you need from me each month?

Mostly quick answers. Once bank feeds are connected, much of the work happens without you. We send a short list of items we cannot identify and ask for receipts or invoices when they matter.

Should my books use cash or accrual basis?

It depends on how you run the business. Cash basis is simpler and common for small service firms. Accrual basis gives a truer view of profit when you send invoices, hold inventory or pay bills on terms. We also check with your tax preparer so the books and the return line up.
