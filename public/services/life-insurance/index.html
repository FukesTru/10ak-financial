Life Insurance Planning for Families and Business Owners in Los Angeles

1Oak Financial Group, based in Monrovia, California, is a tax, accounting and financial planning firm that helps families and owners match life insurance to real needs. We build the coverage amount from your debts, income and goals. We also review policies you already own and explain your choices in plain terms.

Where to begin

What should you gather before deciding how much life insurance you need?

  1. List who depends on you

    Write down who would need money if you died, and for how long. Think of a spouse, children, aging parents or a business partner.

  2. Add up what would need paying

    Include the mortgage, other debts, years of living costs, college goals and final expenses. Then subtract the savings and coverage you already have.

  3. Find every policy you own

    Look for coverage through work, older personal policies and any policy your business owns. Coverage through a job often ends or shrinks when you leave.

  4. Keep your current coverage in place

    Do not cancel anything yet. New coverage is settled only once the insurer approves and issues it, and you accept it.

Situations we see

Which life insurance situations do families and owners bring to us?

Young children and a mortgage

Your family depends on your paycheck for the next 15 to 20 years. Term coverage sized from your actual numbers often fits this need.

Partners without a funded buyout plan

You have a buy-sell agreement, or mean to write one, but nothing set aside to buy out a partner's family.

Estates heavy in real estate or a business

Your estate is valuable but hard to turn into cash quickly. You want your heirs to have choices besides a fast sale.

A permanent policy bought years ago

You still pay on a whole life or universal life policy and are not sure it is on track. Whole life usually has level premiums and a cash value schedule set by the contract. Universal life has flexible premiums, and its internal costs usually rise with age.

Replacing or exchanging a policy

A 1035 exchange moves a policy's value into a new policy or an annuity without income tax on the gain, if the rules are met. A new policy may bring new surrender charges, which are fees for ending it in its early years. Underwriting is the insurer's review of your health and history to set your price, and a new policy repeats it at an older age.

Coverage only through work

Group coverage is a useful benefit, and it is often capped at a multiple of salary. It may end or cost more when you leave the job, retire or sell your company.

How we help

How does 1Oak help you choose and review life insurance?

Term life insurance covers you for a set number of years, often 10 to 30, and then ends. It usually costs the least for a given amount of coverage. Permanent life insurance is designed to last your whole life, as long as it stays funded. It builds cash value, a savings part you can borrow against or withdraw, and it costs much more.

We start with the need and look at products after that. Sometimes the need points to low-cost term coverage. Sometimes it points to permanent coverage for estate or business reasons. Sometimes the right move is to keep exactly what you have. Life insurance is also one part of a wider risk management plan.

  • A needs analysis built from your numbers

    We estimate what your family would need if your income stopped, minus what is already in place. The result is a coverage amount and a time frame.

  • Plain comparisons of term and permanent

    We show what each type provides and where it falls short. You can then weigh a lower premium now against coverage that lasts for life.

  • Business continuity and buy-sell funding

    A buy-sell agreement spells out who buys an owner's share after that owner dies, and at what price. Life insurance is one common way to fund that purchase. We help size the coverage and work with your attorney on the structure.

  • Estate liquidity

    Estate liquidity means having cash ready so heirs can pay taxes, debts and costs without a rushed sale. It matters most when an estate is mostly real estate or a business.

  • Reviews of policies you already own

    We ask the insurer for an in-force illustration, which is its current projection of how your policy may perform. Then we check for underfunding, rising costs and the risk of a lapse. A lapse means the policy ends because premiums or cash value fall short.

  • Beneficiary and ownership checks

    A beneficiary is the person or trust that receives the money. Common problems include an ex-spouse still listed, no backup beneficiary or minor children named directly. Naming minors directly can mean a court gets involved before they receive anything.

California rules

How do California and federal rules affect life insurance planning?

California has no state estate tax or inheritance tax. The federal estate tax applies only to larger estates, and the exempt amount has changed many times. If you own a policy on your own life, its death benefit is usually counted in your estate for federal estate tax. An irrevocable trust is a trust that generally cannot be changed once it is set up. Some people have one own the policy instead, a choice made with an estate attorney.

Death benefits are generally free of federal income tax, with some exceptions, such as certain policies that were sold to someone else. California is a community property state, which means income earned during marriage generally belongs to both spouses. A policy paid for with that income may belong partly to each spouse, even if only one name is on it. California law also gives most individual policies a grace period after a missed payment and lets you name a second person to receive lapse notices.

Questions

What do people ask about life insurance?

How much life insurance do I need?

Enough to cover what your family or business would need, for as long as they would need it, minus what you already have. Rules of thumb based on a multiple of income are only a rough start. They ignore your debts, savings, children's ages and a spouse's earnings.

Should I buy term or permanent life insurance?

Many families use term for needs that end, such as raising children or paying off a mortgage. Permanent coverage is often kept for needs that last, such as estate liquidity or a child with special needs. Universal life policies depend on charges and crediting the insurer can change, so they need regular reviews.

Can I change term coverage to permanent coverage later?

Many term policies let you convert to permanent coverage without a new medical exam, within limits the policy sets. Some policies also carry riders. A rider is an optional add-on, such as one that waives premiums if you become disabled. We check what your policy allows before any deadline passes.

Can you review a policy I already own?

Yes. We request an in-force illustration and compare your premium with what the policy now needs to stay in force. If it is underfunded, options may include paying more, lowering the death benefit or a 1035 exchange. Contact us and have your latest annual statement ready.

How does life insurance help a business with partners?

It can give the surviving owners cash to buy a partner's share, which can help the family receive fair value and the business keep running. Your attorney drafts the agreement. Whether the company or each owner holds the policies has tax and practical effects, so we coordinate that choice with your attorney.

Will I qualify for the price I was quoted?

Not always. Your health, medications, family history, job and hobbies can affect whether you qualify and what you pay. A new policy also starts a contestability period, the window when the insurer can deny a claim over errors in your application. Keep your old policy in force until the new one is approved, issued and accepted.
