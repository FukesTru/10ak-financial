Annuities and Rollovers for Retirement Income in Los Angeles

1Oak Financial Group is a financial planning, tax and accounting firm in Monrovia, California, that helps you weigh annuities and rollovers. An annuity is an insurance contract that trades a sum of your savings for a series of income payments. We explain the costs, limits and taxes in plain terms, so you can decide whether one fits.

Where to begin

What should you do before you roll over a retirement account or buy an annuity?

  1. Add up your must-pay bills

    List housing, food, insurance, health care and taxes. Then line up the income that lasts for life, such as Social Security and any pension. The gap between the 2 tells us whether an annuity is even worth discussing.

  2. Leave the money where it is for now

    A rollover moves money from a workplace plan or IRA into another retirement account. Done the wrong way, it can cause tax withholding or a tax bill. Hold off on any move until the route is clear.

  3. Gather every statement and offer

    Bring your plan statements and any annuity offer you have received. If you already own an annuity, bring its contract and its latest annual statement.

  4. Ask how each person involved is paid

    Annuities are often sold on commission, and that cost is built into the product's price. Ask anyone who suggests one how they are paid, and get the answer in writing.

How we help

How does 1Oak help you weigh an annuity or a rollover?

Many people spend decades saving and very little time planning how to spend it down. Once paychecks stop, the questions change. How much can you spend each month? What if markets fall in your first years of retirement? What if you live to 95?

For some households, lifetime income from an annuity answers those questions well. For others, it adds cost and ties up money that could do more elsewhere. Every income promise in an annuity depends on the insurer's ability to pay claims. We explain the trade-offs and connect the decision to your tax planning.

  • Retirement income mapping

    We line up Social Security, any pension, rental or business income and portfolio withdrawals against your spending, year by year. That shows whether there is a gap worth filling.

  • Rollover planning for 401(k), 403(b) and IRA money

    Before anything moves, we compare what your current plan offers with what you would have after a move. Then we plan a direct transfer so the money avoids needless withholding.

  • Side-by-side contract comparisons

    We lay out an offer's surrender charges, rider fees, crediting limits and income terms next to other choices, including no annuity. A surrender charge is a fee for taking out more than the contract allows in its early years. A rider is an optional add-on feature that usually has its own yearly fee.

  • Reviews of contracts you already own

    We check where an older annuity sits in its surrender period and what its riders cost. A 1035 exchange lets you move one annuity into another without current tax, if the rules are met. It usually starts a new surrender period, so it needs a clear reason.

  • Tax coordination

    We look at how withdrawals or payments will be taxed. We also check how they fit with required minimum distributions, the yearly amounts you must take out of most retirement accounts once you reach a set age. We also weigh Roth conversions and other tax-free options.

  • Spouse and beneficiary choices

    Joint payout options, death benefits and beneficiary forms decide what happens to a contract after you die. We review them with your survivor's income in mind and alongside any life insurance you own.

Situations we see

Which annuity and rollover questions do people bring to us?

Fixed annuities

A fixed annuity pays a stated interest rate for a set number of years. People often compare them with bank certificates of deposit, yet they are insurance contracts and are not FDIC insured. When the rate period ends, the rate resets or you can move the money under the contract's terms.

Fixed indexed annuities

Interest is based partly on how a market index does, within limits the insurer sets and can usually change. A cap is the most you can be credited in a period. In a year the index falls, credited interest is usually zero rather than negative, subject to the contract and the insurer's ability to pay claims. Credits in strong years usually trail the index itself.

Income annuities

You give an insurer a lump sum in exchange for regular payments. They can start within about a year or at a later date you pick. Payments can run for a set time, for your life or for the lives of a couple. Once you commit the money, you usually cannot get it back as a lump sum.

Rollovers after leaving a job

Your old plan may have low costs or features an IRA lacks. One is the age 55 rule, which can allow 401(k) withdrawals without the early penalty if you leave your job in or after the year you turn 55. Company stock in the plan may also qualify for special tax treatment that a rollover would give up.

Pension payments or a lump sum

Some pension plans let you pick a lifetime monthly payment or a single lump sum to roll into an IRA. We compare the 2, including what each means for a surviving spouse.

California rules

How do federal and California rules affect annuities and rollovers?

Annuity earnings are taxed as ordinary income when withdrawn, at the same rates as wages. A 10% federal penalty may also apply to withdrawals before age 59½, with some exceptions. If you bought the annuity with after-tax money, part of each lifetime payment is usually a return of your own money. A deferred annuity is one that grows for years before payments start. Withdrawals from one usually come from earnings first. A step-up in basis resets an asset's tax cost to its value at death. Annuities do not get one, so heirs generally owe income tax on the gains.

Tax-deferred means you owe no tax on growth until you take money out. An annuity inside an IRA or 401(k) adds no extra tax deferral, because the account is already tax-deferred. In a direct trustee-to-trustee transfer, your old plan or IRA sends the money straight to the new one. In a 60-day indirect rollover, the money comes to you first. Anything not redeposited within 60 days is generally taxable and may face the penalty. Indirect rollovers from one IRA to another are limited to 1 in any 12-month period.

California taxes annuity earnings as income too, and it also taxes capital gains as ordinary income. California gives buyers age 60 and older at least 30 days to cancel a new annuity, called a free-look period. Younger buyers usually get a shorter window, printed on the contract. If an insurer fails, a state program may cover part of what it owes, within limits set by law. That program is limited and is no reason to buy any contract.

Questions

What do people ask about annuities and rollovers?

Is an annuity right for me?

It depends on whether Social Security and any pension leave a gap in your must-pay bills. It also depends on how much you value steady income over flexibility. An annuity is usually a poor fit for money you may need soon. We will tell you plainly when one does not fit.

What is the difference between a direct rollover and a 60-day rollover?

In a direct rollover, your old plan sends the money straight to the new account, and no tax is withheld. In a 60-day rollover, the check comes to you, and you must redeposit the full amount within 60 days. An employer plan must withhold 20% for federal tax when it pays you directly. To roll over the full amount, you would need other cash to replace what was withheld.

What are surrender charges and market value adjustments?

Surrender charges apply if you leave a deferred annuity, or take out more than the yearly allowance, during the surrender period. That period often lasts several years, and the charge usually steps down each year. A market value adjustment is a change to your payout based on how interest rates have moved since you bought. It can raise or lower what you receive.

How do inflation and access limits affect an annuity?

A level payment buys a little less each year as prices rise. Some contracts offer yearly increases, which usually means a smaller first payment. Money in an annuity, and especially an income annuity, is hard to get back quickly or cheaply. Keep your emergency fund and near-term cash somewhere easier to reach.

What happens if the insurance company runs into trouble?

Lifetime income and other contract promises rest on the insurer's ability to pay claims. A bank or a federal agency does not back them. We review independent financial strength ratings and may suggest spreading larger amounts across more than one insurer.

Should I hold an annuity inside my IRA?

An IRA already defers tax, so an annuity inside it adds no extra deferral. The reason to do it would have to be a feature such as lifetime income, weighed against the costs and surrender terms. If a pitch for IRA money leans on tax deferral, ask more questions.
