Pension and Cash Balance Plans for Business Owners in Los Angeles

1Oak Financial Group, a Monrovia, California firm that offers tax, accounting and financial planning, helps business owners judge whether a cash balance plan fits. This kind of pension, called a cash balance plan, often lets owners in their 50s and 60s save more than a 401(k) alone allows. We test the yearly commitment against your profits and staff.

Where to begin

What should a business owner check before starting a cash balance plan?

  1. Look back at several years of profit

    Pull your profit for the last 3 to 5 years. These plans suit profits that are high and steady, because a contribution comes due every year.

  2. List everyone on your payroll

    Write down each person's age, pay and hire date. This list is called census data, and it drives what the plan will cost for your staff.

  3. Note what you already save

    Write down what you put into a 401(k) or other plan now. A cash balance plan is usually added alongside a 401(k), so we design them together.

  4. Start the talk well before your tax deadline

    The rules on when a new plan must be set up have loosened in recent years. Starting early leaves time to model a few designs and pick one calmly.

Situations we see

Which owners and businesses look at these plans most?

Medical and dental practices

Practice owners with strong, steady collections who want to build retirement savings faster in the second half of their careers.

Law firms and consultants

Partners and solo professionals with high income and modest overhead who already make the most of their 401(k) each year.

Family business owners catching up

Owners in their 50s or 60s who put profits back into the company for years and now want to save while profits allow.

Owners with a small, younger staff

When owners are older than most employees, a larger share of the total contribution can often go to the owners while the plan still passes testing.

A plan paired with a 401(k) and profit sharing

Many owners run all 3 plans side by side. A profit sharing contribution for staff can help the whole design pass testing.

Owners winding down or selling

A plan that is closing needs careful steps. We help you compare a lump sum with monthly payments and plan the final filings with the administrator.

How we help

How does 1Oak help you set up and run a cash balance plan?

A defined benefit plan is a pension that aims for a set benefit at retirement, and the business pays in what is needed to reach it. A cash balance plan is a version that is easier to follow. Each person has an account that grows by a pay credit and an interest credit every year. A pay credit is a set dollar amount or share of salary. An interest credit is a rate written into the plan.

Each year an actuary sets the range you may contribute. An actuary is a trained professional who uses math about ages, pay and interest rates to work out what a pension needs. Because funding depends mostly on age, pay and plan design, older owners can often put in more. We tie the plan to your tax planning so the deduction is weighed against your other choices for the year.

  • A feasibility study

    We gather your census data and work with an actuary to project contributions for you and your staff under a few different designs.

  • A test against leaner years

    We check the yearly contribution against several years of profit, including a weak one. Through CFO advisory, the contribution can become a planned line in your cash forecast.

  • Staff costs in plain numbers

    Most plans must cover eligible staff and pass nondiscrimination testing. These are federal rules that keep a plan from favoring owners too heavily. We show the cost for employees right next to your own benefit.

  • One set of numbers for every advisor

    A third-party administrator is an outside firm that keeps the plan document, runs the testing and prepares filings. We keep the administrator and the actuary working from the same figures as your books and tax return.

  • Salary and plan design set together

    Contributions are generally deductible to the business, within limits. An S corporation is a company whose profit flows through to the owners' personal returns. For S corporation owners, the plan math uses W-2 wages, so we set your salary and the plan design at the same time.

  • Choices at retirement or sale

    When you retire, sell or close the plan, you may be able to take monthly payments for life or a lump sum. A lump sum can often be moved into an IRA. We map those choices well before you need to make them.

California rules

How do California and federal rules shape a cash balance plan?

Cash balance plans are governed mainly by federal law. The IRS and the Department of Labor set the rules on funding, testing and reporting. Each year the plan files a Form 5500, which is a yearly report to the government. Some plans that cover only owners can file a shorter version. Depending on the business and who is covered, the plan may also owe yearly premiums to a federal agency that insures many private pensions.

California generally follows the federal treatment of contributions to qualified retirement plans, though you should confirm how your own return is affected. The state also requires most employers that offer no retirement plan to sign up for CalSavers, the state savings program. A qualified plan, such as a 401(k) or a cash balance plan, generally meets that rule. If you move out of California in retirement, federal law generally keeps the state from taxing the pension payments you receive.

Questions

What do people ask about pension and cash balance plans?

How much can I put into a cash balance plan each year?

It depends mainly on your age, your pay and the plan's design. The federal limits behind the math are adjusted from time to time. Owners closer to retirement can often put in more, because there are fewer years left to fund the benefit. The actuary works out your allowed range every year.

What if the plan's investments do better or worse than the interest credit?

Each account grows by the interest credit written into the plan. If plan assets fall short of that rate, the business generally makes up the gap over time. If assets run well ahead, future contributions may shrink. A large surplus can be costly to unwind when the plan ends.

Can I freeze or end the plan later?

Plans can be amended, and future benefits can be frozen with proper notice to participants. Benefits people have already earned are protected. Ending a plan after only a few years without a real business reason may draw IRS questions. We review any change with your administrator and actuary before it happens.

Is a cash balance plan a fit if my income swings a lot?

It may be a hard fit. Contributions are required every year within the actuary's range, even in a slow year. If profits rise and fall sharply, a more flexible plan, such as a 401(k) with profit sharing, may be a better place to start.

How does this plan fit with Roth accounts and other tax-free savings?

Cash balance contributions are pre-tax. The business deducts them now, and payouts are taxed as income later. Many owners balance that with Roth accounts or other tax-free investment choices. The right mix depends on the tax rate you expect in retirement.
