Strategic Risk Management for Business Owners and Families in Los Angeles

1Oak Financial Group, a Monrovia, California tax, accounting and financial planning firm, helps owners and families see all their risks in one place. We review the coverage you already have, find gaps and weigh options with you. Then we work with your own insurance agent and attorney, who can make any changes.

Where to begin

Where should you start with a risk review?

  1. Gather your declarations pages

    A declarations page is the summary at the front of a policy that lists what is covered, how much and for whom. Pull one for each home, auto and business policy. Add any umbrella policy, which adds extra liability coverage above your other policies. Your agent can usually send copies.

  2. Find your business papers

    Bring the papers that set up your company, any buy-sell agreement and any loan terms that require coverage. A buy-sell agreement is a contract that says what happens to an owner's share if that owner dies, becomes disabled or leaves.

  3. Make a rough list of what you own and owe

    Include homes, rentals, the business, investment accounts and major debts. This list shows what a lawsuit or a large loss could reach.

  4. Note what has changed lately

    A new home, a new partner, a teen driver, new hires or a renewal notice with higher costs all count. Gaps often appear right after changes like these.

Situations we see

Which risk situations come up most often?

Owners whose income depends on showing up

Doctors, contractors, attorneys and other owner-operators whose revenue slows fast if they are out for a few months.

Families with visible wealth

Households with rental property, teen drivers or a large net worth, where 1 lawsuit could go beyond standard policy limits.

Businesses that lean on a few people

Companies where losing 1 person would hurt sales, operations or lender relationships, and where the buy-sell agreement has not been updated in years.

Homeowners in fire or earthquake zones

Foothill and canyon homeowners who have had a policy dropped at renewal, or who are unsure what their coverage would pay after a large loss.

Retirees planning for a long life

Outliving your savings, a market drop early in retirement and long-term care costs belong on the list too. Some people cover part of their spending with lifetime income, while others keep larger cash reserves.

How we help

How does 1Oak help you see and manage your risks?

Most people buy coverage one policy at a time, from different agents, in different years. Gaps show up between those policies. Your umbrella policy may not line up cleanly with the home and auto policies beneath it. A rental held in a limited liability company may not be named on any policy at all.

We start with a list of what could go wrong and what each event could cost you. For each serious risk there are 4 choices. You can avoid it, reduce it, transfer it to an insurer or by contract, or keep it because you can absorb the cost. With your permission, we share what we find with your agent and attorney so they can adjust policies or documents.

  • Disability and lost income

    For many owners, the ability to work is the most valuable thing they have. We look at what happens to household and business bills if you cannot work for months. Disability insurance replaces part of your income if illness or injury keeps you from working.

  • Lawsuits and liability limits

    We compare your umbrella limits with what you have at stake. For the business, we look at professional liability, which covers claims that your work harmed a client. We also look at employment practices liability, which covers claims such as wrongful firing or harassment.

  • Key people and ownership

    If a partner or key employee died or became disabled, could the business keep going? We review how a buyout would be paid for, alongside any life insurance you carry for that purpose.

  • Cyber attacks and payment fraud

    A fake wire request or a data breach can be costly for a small company. We check what your current policies would cover and suggest controls, such as a phone call to confirm any change in payment details. Tighter approvals can also be part of CFO advisory work.

  • Too much riding on one thing

    Concentration risk means a large share of your income or wealth depends on one client, one stock or one building. We measure it, and the same figure appears in our financial ratio analysis.

  • Overlaps and gaps across your policies

    We read the limits, the exclusions and the list of who is covered on each policy. An exclusion is something a policy says it will not pay for. We show where coverage overlaps, where it falls short and where you may be paying for protection you no longer need.

California rules

How do California conditions affect your risk plan?

Standard homeowners and business property policies in California generally exclude earthquake damage. Earthquake coverage is usually bought separately, and it often carries a large deductible. A deductible is the amount you pay before the policy pays. Wildfire risk has also made coverage harder to find, or more costly, in some foothill and canyon areas. We help you and your agent weigh each gap against the cost of closing it.

California generally requires every employer with employees to carry workers' compensation insurance. Workers' compensation pays for job-related injuries and illness among your staff. State law also sets notice rules before an insurer declines to renew a homeowners policy. After some large wildfires, the state has paused nonrenewals for a time in nearby areas. These rules change, so we confirm them with your agent before you act.

Questions

What do people ask about strategic risk management?

Which risks do business owners most often miss?

Disability comes up most. Many owners insure their life and their property but not their ability to earn. Key-person risk, employee claims, payment fraud and wealth tied up in the business are close behind.

How is risk management different from buying insurance?

Buying insurance answers which policy to purchase. Risk management first asks whether a risk can be avoided or reduced. Then it asks whether to transfer the risk to an insurer or keep it. Sometimes the answer is a better contract, stronger controls or a cash reserve.

Do you replace my insurance agent or attorney?

No. We work alongside the advisors you already have. With your permission, we share what the review finds so your agent can adjust policies and your attorney can update documents. Our role is to keep the whole picture in view.

How often should my coverage be reviewed?

Once a year is a sensible habit, ideally a couple of months before your main policies renew. A review also makes sense after you buy property, hire staff, add a partner or add a teen driver. In parts of California where home coverage is harder to get, read each renewal notice closely.

What if I am missing some of my policy papers?

That is common, and it is fine to start with what you have. Your agent can usually send declarations pages on request. If you are unsure what to ask for, contact us and we will help you make a short list.
