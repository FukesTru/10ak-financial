CFO Advisory Services for Growing Businesses in Los Angeles

1Oak Financial Group is a tax, accounting and financial planning firm in Monrovia, California, that can act as a part-time chief financial officer, or CFO, for growing businesses. We build cash forecasts, budgets and simple reports. Then we meet with you each month, so choices about hiring, pricing and borrowing rest on your own numbers.

Where to begin

What should you do first before bringing in a part-time CFO?

  1. Write down the questions that keep coming up

    Can we afford 2 more hires? Which clients make us money? Will cash run short this summer? Your list shapes where the work starts.

  2. Make sure your books are current

    Forecasts built on old or wrong numbers can point you the wrong way. If your books are behind, catch them up first through our accounting and bookkeeping service or with your own bookkeeper.

  3. Gather 2 years of statements and your loans

    Pull your profit and loss statements, balance sheets and tax returns, plus a list of loans. These show where the business has been.

  4. Note the big decisions ahead

    A new location, a loan request, a key hire or a price change are all easier to judge with numbers worked out ahead of time.

Situations we see

Which situations bring owners to CFO advisory?

Payroll growing faster than reporting

You have added people, sites or services faster than your financial reports have kept up.

A loan or credit line on the way

A line of credit is a loan you can draw on and repay as needed, up to a set limit. If you plan to apply for one, an equipment loan or a loan on a business building, you want projections a lender can follow.

Sales up, profit flat

Revenue keeps growing, yet profit has not followed. A profit breakdown and a pricing review can help show where margin is slipping.

Month-end spent in spreadsheets

If closing each month means a weekend of building forecasts, handing that work off lets you get back to running the company.

Risks that need a closer look

Relying on a single large customer or keeping little cash on hand can leave a business exposed. We point these out, and our strategic risk management work looks at them in more depth.

An owner thinking about a future sale

Clean history and a clear view of what drives profit help when you start talking with buyers. A buy-sell agreement sets what happens to an owner's share if they leave, and your attorney drafts it.

How we help

How does 1Oak work as your part-time CFO?

Growing companies often reach a stage where the books are right but the owner still cannot answer the big questions. Those questions land on someone who already has a full-time job running the business.

A fractional CFO is a chief financial officer who works for you part time, for a set number of hours or days each month. We build the forecasts, reports and analysis a full-time CFO would, while your bookkeeper keeps handling the daily records. You still make the decisions, and we make sure the right numbers are in front of you when you do.

  • A rolling 13-week cash forecast

    This is a week-by-week view of cash coming in and going out over the next 3 months. Each week, the oldest week drops off and a new one is added. A tight payroll week or a slow-paying customer can show up before it becomes a problem.

  • A budget and monthly comparisons

    We build a yearly budget with you, compare actual results with it each month and update the full-year outlook as things change.

  • A short dashboard of key numbers

    A key performance indicator, or KPI, is a number you track to see how the business is doing. We pick a handful that fit your business, such as gross margin, which is the share of sales left after direct costs. We track them monthly so trends are easier to spot.

  • Profit by service, client or location

    We break results down to show where the business earns its profit. The answers can shape choices about pricing, staffing and which work to pursue.

  • Pricing and margin review

    When costs rise or you launch something new, we model how changes in price, volume and labor may affect your margin. Pricing then rests on your own numbers.

  • Reports your lender can follow

    Banks often ask for statements, projections, a list of debts and covenant figures. A covenant is a condition in a loan agreement, such as keeping cash flow above a set level. We prepare clear packages and use financial ratio analysis where it helps explain the numbers.

California rules

How do California rules affect cash forecasts and hiring plans?

A new hire in California costs more than the salary alone. Employers generally must carry workers' compensation insurance, which pays for job injuries, and must provide paid sick leave. State payroll taxes add to the cost as well. Our hiring models include these items so the full cost shows up before you commit.

Minimum wage rules also vary by place. Some cities in Los Angeles County set their own minimum wage above the state rate, and these rates often change each year. We check the rate that applies where your staff work.

Tax dates belong in the cash forecast too. Most LLCs and corporations doing business here owe California a minimum tax each year, even in a slow year. Owners also make estimated tax payments, which are prepayments of income tax on profit that has no withholding. California asks for more of these in the first half of the year, which can pull cash out of the business in spring. We line these dates up with your tax planning so they are part of the forecast.

Questions

What do people ask about cfo advisory services?

How is a part-time CFO different from my bookkeeper?

Your bookkeeper records what happened, such as each transaction and the monthly close. A CFO uses those records to look ahead, such as where cash will be in 8 weeks and which choices the numbers support. The CFO work depends on accurate books, so both roles matter.

What size business is this for?

There is no strict cutoff by sales or headcount. Common signs include a real payroll to fund, a loan on the horizon or a new location under review. Another is an owner who spends more time on finance than on the business. If a few of these sound familiar, it may be worth a conversation.

When does a full-time CFO make more sense?

A full-time hire usually fits once finance work fills a full week on its own. That can happen when you run several companies, raise outside money often or must produce audited statements, which are checked by outside accountants. We will tell you when you are getting close and help define the role.

How often will we meet?

Engagements are usually built around a monthly review, with shorter check-ins when a cash question or a decision comes up. During budget season or a loan request, we usually talk more often. We agree on the rhythm at the start and adjust it as your needs change.

Can you help us get a bank loan?

We can help you prepare for one with projections, a list of debts, clean statements and a plain account of how the money will be used and repaid. The lender makes the credit decision, so no one can promise an approval. A well-organized request is often easier for a lender to review.

How accurate are cash forecasts?

A forecast is an informed estimate built on assumptions and your accounting data. Actual results will differ, sometimes by a lot, when customers, costs or interest rates move. We update the forecast often so it tracks what is really happening.
